# Changes made since Part II
> Include few paragraphs that reflects on changes you made since the completion of Part II. 

Since completing Part II, I made several revisions to strengthen both the clarity and impact of my data story. One of the most significant changes was refining the narrative structure to create a more compelling and logical flow. I reorganized sections so that the story moves from broad context about the importance of music education into more specific data about inequities in access, ultimately leading to a clear and persuasive call to action.

I also revised my data visualizations to improve readability and accessibility. This included simplifying charts, using more consistent color schemes, and ensuring that each visual directly supported the point being made in the accompanying text. In earlier drafts, some visuals felt disconnected from the narrative. I worked to better integrate them so that they guide the reader through the story rather than interrupt it.

Additionally, I strengthened my call to action by making it more concise and directly tied to my intended audience of future policymakers. I focused on making the takeaway actionable and urgent, emphasizing the need for sustained and equitable funding for music education.

## The audience
> Talk about who you identified as the audience for your final data story.  Include any other information you've used that helped you narrow the focus (e.g. insights from your interviews, personas, etc.).  Note any specific adjustments you made to your final project to make it work for your audience.

The primary audience for my data story is future policymakers and arts advocates, particularly those who will have influence over education funding decisions. This includes students in policy, arts management, and education fields, as well as emerging leaders who may shape legislation or institutional priorities.

I narrowed this audience based on both course themes and the broader goal of my project, to advocate for systemic change in access to music education. Insights from class discussions and research reinforced that funding decisions are a key driver of inequity, so I tailored my story to individuals who could realistically impact those decisions.

To better serve this audience, I made specific adjustments to my project. I emphasized clear, evidence based arguments and avoided overly technical language so that the content remains accessible while still being persuasive. I also ensured that the data directly supports policy related conclusions, framing music education not just as an artistic benefit but as a critical component of student success and equity.

## Final design decisions
> You can specifically break out your design decisions here, or include it under *Changes made since Part II* and delete this section. Talk about the design decisions you had to make along the way, and reflect on anything in particular that stands out to you that you learned working through the process.  Include any other information that helps round out your data story. 

Throughout the process, I made intentional design choices to enhance both engagement and comprehension. One key decision was to prioritize a clean, visually cohesive layout that allows the data to stand out without overwhelming the reader. I used consistent typography and color schemes to create a unified look and to subtly guide the reader’s attention.

Another important design decision was pacing. I used a combination of short text sections and visuals to maintain reader engagement, ensuring that the story feels dynamic rather than text heavy. This was especially important given the digital storytelling format of Shorthand, where scrolling and visual flow play a major role in user experience.

I also learned the importance of aligning design with purpose. Rather than including visuals simply for aesthetic appeal, I focused on making sure each element contributed meaningfully to the story. This process helped me better understand how design and data work together to communicate complex ideas effectively.
